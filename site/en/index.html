P.01PUBLIC / ANDROID

WEARABLE BIOSENSING

ThermoPatch

Maps BLE temperature sensors to six body locations, shows readings and connection state on Android, and connects Firebase records with device-specific DFU workflows.

EvidenceArchived app screen and v1.0.1 checks. One populated location does not establish simultaneous six-point acquisition.

  • Kotlin
  • BLE
  • Firebase
  • nRF52840
Purpose, approach, and scope
Purpose
Observe temperature at multiple body locations and maintain sensing patches from a phone.
Approach
Jetpack Compose, ViewModels, and a BLE client manage device state and readings. Nordic DFU requires explicit target-device selection before an update.
Scope
The public version includes live readings, body-location mapping, and updates. Long-term history and firmware-version compatibility checks remain limited. Research use, not diagnosis.

P.02PUBLIC / IMAGING

BIOLOGICAL IMAGING

SpaceCellCam

Schedules QSXGA JPEG capture with an ESP32-S3 and OV5640, coordinating illumination, first-frame focus locking, sensor standby and microSD storage.

EvidencePublic capture sequence and an archived integration photograph. The period is a setting, not a new endurance measurement.

  • C++
  • ESP32-S3
  • OV5640
  • microSD
Purpose, approach, and scope
Purpose
Record biological experiments while reducing repeated autofocus and extended illumination.
Approach
Reuse the initial focus lock, copy JPEG data to PSRAM, release the frame buffer, and enter camera standby before writing a temporary file, renaming it, and checking its size.
Scope
The public core is capture and storage firmware. Focus, heat, and power behavior depend on the actual optical setup; no completed flight or environmental qualification is claimed.

P.03PUBLIC / RTL

RELIABILITY LOGIC

Space BMC Core

Implements a parameterized Verilog heartbeat timeout monitor with a self-checking testbench for timeout, heartbeat clearing, explicit clearing and asynchronous reset.

EvidenceThe saved simulation reports 15 checks and 0 errors. A Recovery FSM is not implemented.

  • Verilog
  • Questa
  • DE10
Purpose, approach, and scope
Purpose
Define heartbeat-loss timeouts in clock cycles as a foundation for system monitoring logic.
Approach
Latch the timeout output; reset, a sampled heartbeat, or clear_timeout clears it and restarts the interval. Run small-parameter, cycle-accurate automated checks in Questa.
Scope
The current milestone excludes recovery sequencing and a Recovery FSM. Functional simulation does not establish board operation, timing closure, or space-grade validation.

P.04PUBLIC / ZEPHYR

LOW-POWER SENSING

nRF54L15 × MLX90614

Reads MLX90614 object and ambient temperature in Zephyr and sends readings over BLE, combining a 60-second sampling interval with phone-triggered immediate sampling.

EvidenceThe public README documents wiring, the GATT contract and immediate sampling, with a separate power-budget analysis.

  • C
  • Zephyr
  • nRF54L15
Purpose, approach, and scope
Purpose
Schedule intermittent sensing and low-power waiting while retaining phone connectivity and control.
Approach
Read ambient and object temperatures over I²C, expose BLE read/notify and measurement-control characteristics, and wait on semaphore timeouts. UART and diagnostic LEDs are disabled by default.
Scope
System ON idle retains BLE; this is not System OFF. Battery-life estimates, measured current, and measurement accuracy must be interpreted separately.

05 / PROFILE

Cross-disciplinary training.
Applied R&D focus.

From biomechatronics and biomedical engineering to electrical engineering, my research path consistently centers on sensing, control, digital design, and hardware-software integration.

CURRENT FOCUS

Wearable devices × Space research systems

I independently develop my doctoral and recent R&D projects, with responsibility for requirements, architecture, firmware and applications, integration, and test documentation according to each project's scope. Completed work and ongoing development are identified separately.

My training began in biomechatronic engineering at National Ilan University, followed by a biomedical engineering master's degree and doctoral study in electrical engineering at NYCU. This path connects biological experiments with sensing, control, and hardware-software development.

I work across Nordic nRF, ESP32, Raspberry Pi, Android/Wear OS, and FPGA platforms. Beyond acquiring data, I address offline storage, device identity, synchronization, and fault records so experimental workflows are easier to operate and trace.

The later ThermoPatch V2 platform uses Room for local history and per-device sync progress. BLE retrieval and Firebase upload are separated, with pending queues, background retries, and round-robin device sync. V2 and sweat-pressure research code remain private; only their purpose and engineering work are described here.

My main research directions are low-power wearable devices and systems for space biomedical experiments, covering multi-device BLE data workflows, microfluidic payloads, imaging and control. Verilog RTL, edge computing and TinyML are engineering methods I continue to develop. I document design choices alongside test conditions, simulation results and unfinished work.
